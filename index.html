Space heat math

Heater season goes wrong in three places: the watts guessed from the box photo instead of the room, the breaker trusted because the plug fits instead of because the arithmetic holds, and the bill discovered in February instead of in October. Space heat math turns the room into the watts, turns the watts into the breaker check, and turns the hours into the bill. Exact arithmetic, every borrowed number labeled.

Open the calculators

The three tools

  • Watt plan - the room area and the ceiling height become the watts the room actually needs.
  • Circuit plan - the watts against the breaker become the honest answer on what the outlet can carry.
  • Cost plan - the watts and the daily hours become the kilowatt-hours and the month on the bill.

Labeled norms

Labeled norms: 100 W per m2 at a labeled 2.4 m ceiling, 120 V mains, the 80% continuous-load rule, and 25 cents a kWh - all illustrative defaults you can change by measuring. A plug-in heater belongs in the wall outlet, never a power strip or an extension cord, with a clear meter around it and off when the room is empty. Real warmth starts with socks and sealed windows; the heater is the last meter of comfort, not the first.
